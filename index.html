How it works

Decide once, while things are calm.

The hardest moments are rarely the moments we planned for. Rafael lets you make the decision ahead of time and then keeps it for you.

  1. 1

    Choose what to block

    Pick installed apps and add websites or supported categories. Decide which essential apps stay available, such as your dialer, maps or work apps.

  2. 2

    Set your end date

    Choose an exact date and time. Before you confirm, Rafael shows the full summary: what's blocked, what stays open, your time zone and how strongly your phone can protect it.

  3. 3

    It stays locked

    After you activate, there's no pause, shortcut or paid bypass inside Rafael. Your setup code can't bring the end date forward. Emergency calling keeps working.

  4. 4

    It ends automatically

    At the deadline, restrictions lift on their own. You don't need a code, a payment or a connection, and starting another commitment is always a new, fresh choice.

Calm, not shame

A steady reminder of the choice you made.

When you reach something you've blocked, Rafael doesn't lecture you. It reminds you of the choice you made, shows when it ends, and points you to what's still open.

No labels, no diagnosis and no guilt. Rafael is one tool that helps you keep a decision. It doesn't claim to cure anything.

Honest limits

Strong, but we won't call it unbreakable.

Rafael itself won't accept early-unlock requests. How hard it is to get around Rafael from outside the app depends on your phone and how it's set up. Here's how things stand.

Android first

We're building and testing on Android first, starting with a prototype that measures how well blocking actually holds up. A stricter managed setup may offer stronger protection on some devices.

iPhone later

We'll evaluate iPhone separately. Apple's tools work differently and permission can be withdrawn, so we won't promise the same strength there.

Device-level escapes

A factory reset, system recovery, or a rooted or jailbroken phone can defeat app-level controls. On a standard install, changing permissions or settings can weaken protection. If protection is lost, Rafael will tell you rather than show a false "protected" status.

Website filtering isn't perfect

Category lists can miss new sites or block the wrong ones. Filtering can't always tell an unwanted page from a useful one on the same site, and it doesn't remove downloaded files. Another VPN can also conflict with it.

If you're finding things hard, it can help to talk to someone you trust or a local support service as well. Rafael works best as one part of your own plan.

Privacy by design

What you block is your business.

Your blocked apps, websites and commitment details stay on your device by default. Rafael doesn't need an account to keep an active commitment running.

  • Stays on your device. Your selections and commitment details are stored locally.
  • No browsing logs. We don't keep full URLs, page contents or detailed browsing activity.
  • No snooping on secure traffic. Filtering avoids decrypting your personal HTTPS connections.
  • Your code is protected. Only a secure verifier is stored, never the code itself.
  • Analytics leave out the sensitive parts. They never include the apps you chose or the sites you visit.
  • Sharing is off by default. Nothing goes to anyone unless you choose to send it.

Questions

Good to know

Can I end a commitment early?

No. That's the point. You can edit or cancel freely while you're still setting it up. Once you activate it, the end date is fixed. Rafael shows a clear summary and asks you to confirm before that point.

What if there's an emergency?

Emergency calling stays available throughout, along with the essential apps you choose before you activate.

What if my subscription ends?

An active commitment, and emergency access, never depends on payment. Your commitment ends at its deadline either way.

When can I use it?

Rafael is in development. Android comes first, after testing on real devices. Join the waitlist to hear when there's something to try.

Is Rafael religious?

The name is inspired by Raphael, who is associated with healing, and green reflects that. You don't need any faith to use Rafael.
