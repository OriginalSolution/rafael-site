How it works

Three steps. Then it holds.

  1. 1

    Choose what to block

    Pick apps from your phone, type in websites like bet365.com, and add phone numbers you don't want to reach you. Keep the people who matter always reachable.

  2. 2

    Set your time

    Swipe through simple cards: 1 day, 1 week, 30 days or your own date. Set a 6-digit setup PIN so changes stay deliberate.

  3. 3

    Pledge and hold to start

    Read your pledge, then press and hold for two seconds to seal it. From then on there is no pause, cancel or early unlock until it ends.

Features

Built to keep your promise.

  • Apps

    Betting apps, scores apps, social apps: when you open one, Rafael's shield covers it.

  • Websites

    A private on-device filter blocks the sites you list, subdomains included, in any browser.

  • Phone numbers

    Block calls from numbers that pull you back, and keep chosen numbers always reachable.

  • Per-app times

    Betting apps blocked all day, YouTube only 10 pm to 6 am, or weekdays only. Each item gets its own schedule.

    Rafael never blocks emergency calls, your phone app or system settings.

    On big inner screens like the Galaxy Z Fold, Rafael splits into two panes.

For parents

Help your child take back control.

You can set Rafael up on your child's phone to keep gambling, adult sites and the apps that pull them in out of reach for a set time. Everything runs on that phone, and the rules you agree on stay in place until the end time.

  1. 1

    Talk first, then set it up together

    Rafael works best as a shared promise, not a secret. Sit down with your child, agree on what gets blocked and for how long, and do the setup on their phone together.

  2. 2

    Choose what to block

    Pick betting, gaming or social apps from the phone's app list. Type in the gambling and adult websites to block, one by one (subdomains are included). Add numbers that shouldn't get through.

  3. 3

    Set times that fit their day

    Block some things all the time and others only at set times: school hours on weekdays, or bedtime with an overnight window like 10 pm to 6 am.

  4. 4

    Keep your numbers reachable

    Mark your own number, and anyone else who matters, as always reachable. Emergency calls always work, whatever is blocked.

  5. 5

    Choose the setup PIN yourself

    Choose the 6-digit setup PIN yourself and keep it. It confirms the commitment, and it can never end or shorten one.

  6. 6

    Start a timed commitment

    Pick a day, a week, 30 days or your own end date, then hold to start. Nobody can end it early, you included. Blocks can only be added or made stricter, and everything is released automatically at the end time.

! Honest limits for parents

  • It isn't a monitoring tool. Rafael runs only on your child's phone. There's no remote dashboard, and you can't see their activity or get reports.
  • It isn't tamper-proof. A determined teen with access to Settings could get around it, for example by removing permissions, changing VPN or Private DNS settings, using safe mode, reinstalling or doing a factory reset. Rafael shows on the phone when protection is weakened.
  • Calls only. Texts from blocked numbers still arrive.
  • A short delay. A blocked app can show for a moment before the block screen covers it.
  • No early unlock for you either. Choose the length carefully. You can't shorten it later.

Start short, a day or a week, and talk about how it went before choosing a longer commitment.

Honest limits

What Rafael can and can't do.

Rafael makes slipping a lot harder, but no blocker is impossible to get around. Someone determined can still find a way, for example by wiping the phone or using another device. Rafael works best as a firm promise you made on a clear-headed day.

  • Texts from blocked numbers aren't blocked, only calls.
  • With the standard method, a blocked app can flash for a moment before the shield covers it. Turning on the optional faster blocking makes it instant.
  • Rafael is a self-help tool, not a medical or treatment service. If you're struggling, please also reach out to someone you trust or a local support line.
Android says the app is blocked or unsafe. What do I do?

Until Rafael is on Google Play, you install it from a file, and Play Protect may warn you because it doesn't know the app yet. Tap More details, then Install anyway. Each release lists its SHA-256 fingerprint on GitHub so you can check the file.

It won't install on my Samsung.

Samsung's Auto Blocker stops installs from files. Open Settings, then Security and privacy, then Auto Blocker, and turn it off while you install. You can turn it back on afterwards.

A switch is greyed out in Settings ("restricted setting").

On Android 13 and newer, apps installed from a file need a one-time unlock. Rafael walks you through it: open Settings, then Apps, then Rafael, tap ⋮ at the top right, choose Allow restricted settings and confirm. If you don't see it, tap the grey switch once first, then go back. This step goes away once Rafael is on Google Play.

Can I end a commitment early?

No. That's the point. There's no pause, cancel or early unlock until the time you chose. You can still add more blocks or make times stricter. Emergency calls, your phone app and system settings always stay available.

What does Rafael know about me?

Nothing leaves your phone. There's no account, no sign-up, no ads and no analytics. Your lists, schedules and PIN hash are stored on the device only. Read the privacy policy for the details.

Can I use Rafael on my child's phone?

Yes. Set it up together on their phone: choose what to block, set times like school hours or bedtime, keep your number always reachable, and choose the setup PIN yourself. It runs only on that phone, so there's no remote dashboard and no activity reports.

As a parent, can I end it early or check what my child does?

No to both. Nobody can end a commitment early, parents included. You can only add blocks or make times stricter until it ends. Rafael doesn't record or send anyone your child's activity, so it can't show you what they do.

Does it work on iPhone?

Not yet. Rafael is Android first. Google Play is next, then we'll look at iPhone.
